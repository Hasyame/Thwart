Privacy policy

Thwart is made by one person, Benoît Breul, and this page says what it does with your information. Short version: almost nothing, and none of it reaches me.

Last updated: 22 August 2026.

What stays on your phone

Everything you make in the app:

  • Your decks, and which packs you own
  • Your campaigns, and every choice recorded in them
  • Your play history, times, scores and notes
  • Photographs you take of the table
  • Your settings

None of it is uploaded. There is no account to make, no server of mine for it to go to, and no copy of it anywhere but your phone. If you uninstall the app, it is gone (which is why the app offers a backup file you can save yourself).

Photographs are kept in the app's private storage, not in your gallery, and other apps cannot read them.

What leaves your phone

Three things, and two of them only if you ask.

Card data, from MarvelCDB. The app downloads card information from marvelcdb.com so it can show you cards, and it fetches card images while you browse. These are ordinary web requests. Nothing about you is attached to them, and nothing is sent to MarvelCDB. Like any web request, MarvelCDB's own servers will see the request arriving, which includes your IP address. Their privacy practices are theirs, not mine.

BoardGameGeek, only if you set it up. The app can send a finished game to your BoardGameGeek account. This is off unless you turn it on and enter your own credentials. Those credentials are encrypted with a key held in the Android keystore and never leave your phone except to log in to BoardGameGeek itself. I never see them. If you never touch this setting, the app never contacts BoardGameGeek.

A crash report, only if you send it. If the app crashes, it writes what went wrong to a file on your phone. Nothing is sent automatically. If you choose to report it, the app opens your own email app with that file attached, and you decide whether to press send. You can read exactly what you are sending before you send it.

What the app does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising networks
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting service
  • No tracking of any kind, and no advertising identifier
  • No account, no sign-up, no profile
  • No selling or sharing of your data, because there is none to sell
  • No location, no contacts, no microphone

Permissions

The app asks for one permission: internet access, used to download card data and images, and for BoardGameGeek if you enable it.

It does not ask for camera permission. When you photograph the table, the app hands the job to your phone's own camera app and receives the picture back.

Android's automatic cloud backup is switched off for this app, so your data is not copied to Google Drive without your knowledge.

Children

Thwart is a companion for a card game and is not directed at children under 13. It collects nothing from anyone, children included.

Changes
