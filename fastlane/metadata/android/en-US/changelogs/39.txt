Photographs of the table, a way to put a game away, and Sinister Motives.

You can photograph the table during a game. The pictures stay with the play and
show up in your stats. They are taken by the phone's own camera app, so the app
asks for no camera permission and never opens a camera itself, and they live in
the app's private storage: a picture of your living room does not belong in the
shared gallery, and there is nowhere else for it to go.

A game can now be put away instead of abandoned. The page writes down what you
will not remember in a week: a photo of the table, whether you stopped in the
player phase or the villain phase and which of its five steps, what each hero
has left, and the villain's life and which stage is face up. Everything on it is
optional. The game waits for you on the Play tab, and opening it shows the note
back, with the photo.

Sinister Motives is finished. The reputation track is the printed one, node for
node, and the app keeps it: it asks the six conditions after each scenario, does
the adding, and shows only the boxes the track has reached. It also deals what
the rules deal at random, so you do not have to: the Community Service side
scheme, the Osborn Tech attachments, and each player's three S.H.I.E.L.D. Tech
upgrades, one draw per player rather than one for the table.

Two French terms are corrected. The four card groupings are affinites, which is
what the printed cards and the reference glossary call them, and the villain is
a mechant throughout instead of a vilain in some places and a mechant in others.
