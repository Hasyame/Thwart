Deckbuilding, and Fear No Evil put back on the book.

A new deck now opens with the hero's own cards already in it, at their printed
quantities. They were never optional, so a deck that opened empty just failed
validation until you added by hand what the rules had already chosen.

Every card row carries a mark for its type — shape as well as colour, so the
list can be skimmed by someone who cannot rely on colour. Lists sort by type,
cost or name; cards with no printed cost sort last rather than as zero.

Fear No Evil had a dead end: losing a scenario left you on a page that said
"Campaign complete" with nowhere to go. Beyond fixing that, three rules are now
right. Losing a scenario does not fail it — you can go straight back and try
again, and it is choosing to move on that turns the environment over to its
ÉCHOUÉ face and takes the job out of the campaign. The rotation never asked you
to keep one of the two environments it draws; it draws two and pushes both, and
the page says so instead of asking. And retrying a job faced a different
subordinate every time, because finishing a scenario dropped the villain the
campaign had noted against it.

Each scenario now says what its ending did, naming the subordinate and the
environment, with a retry, continue, take a break and stop the campaign.

An unfinished campaign is marked (WIP) in the chooser. Fear No Evil and
Sinister Motives both are.

Sinister Motives joins the campaigns, in English, with its reputation track:
the app asks the six conditions after each scenario, does the adding, and shows
only the track boxes you have reached. It also draws the Community Service side
scheme and the Osborn Tech attachments for you.
